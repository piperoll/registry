- `id`: PIR-2026-0103
- `title`: Two coordinated disclosures. METR and Redwood Research (2026-10-22): one employee from each spent 2026-07-21 to 08-11 inside OpenAI's infrastructure stress-testing its internal misalignment monitoring. Findings: "roughly 75-94% of agentic internal Responses API traffic from models at least as strong as GPT-5.5 was eligible for monitoring" (99.2% for Codex); between May and July 2026 "approximately 10% of eligible internal Codex traffic went unmonitored" because compaction events were not monitored, so "agents could indefinitely postpone being monitored by simply continuing to take actions"; RL training was not covered at the start; "a way Codex agents could in principle bypass Codex sandboxing and Auto-Review, allowing them to take any action that the host user could take"; "an exploit agents could use that would prevent hundreds of recent actions (including the actions involved in the exploit itself) from being monitored" (details redacted by OpenAI); inputs that crash the monitor; "two independent client-controllable headers that disable the monitoring system for new agents"; two supposedly read-only tools usable for arbitrary sandbox actions. OpenAI says all production-affecting findings are patched; METR "have not evaluated these claims". OpenAI (2026-10-23, "Securing our frontier model training environment"): its own frontier-agent red-team found "zero-day vulnerabilities, virtual machine escapes, and hardware side-channels", including a cache-timing channel between isolated microVMs and chained escapes that led to patches in Kata Containers, gVisor, Moby/Docker and the Linux kernel; frontier training now runs with no internet access, paused 2026-09-20 and resumed 10-01

### The failure
- `root_cause`: operator-error (monitoring triggered only when agents returned control to users, leaving compaction unmonitored; training uncovered; sandbox and review bypasses; client-controllable headers disabling monitoring; isolation components with escapable flaws). The red-team findings are demonstrated capability, not acts

- `mechanism`: METR and Redwood, 2026-10-22: a three-week pilot (07-21 to 08-11) by one employee from each organisation, "a pilot for giving external researchers deep access" rather than a full audit, producing a 61-page internal report and a 38-page version redacted by OpenAI. Coverage: 75 to 94% of agentic Responses API traffic from models at least GPT-5.5-strong was eligible for monitoring, 99.2% for Codex; monitoring fired only when an agent returned control to the user, so compaction events went unmonitored and "approximately 10% of eligible internal Codex traffic went unmonitored" between May and July 2026, a gap OpenAI had found independently and fixed during the exercise; RL training runs were not covered at the start. Evasion: a Codex sandbox and Auto-Review bypass giving "any action that the host user could take"; an exploit, redacted, that would hide "hundreds of recent actions (including the actions involved in the exploit itself)"; inputs that make the monitor fail by exploiting differences between what the agent and the monitor see of a trajectory; two client-controllable headers that disable monitoring for new agents; two tools believed read-only that allowed arbitrary sandbox actions (not validated). Monitor error rate under 0.2% and no load-shedding in a sampled day. OpenAI told them "all of the vulnerabilities we identified that impact their production safeguards stack have been patched"; METR "have not evaluated these claims" and "lacked ability to check whether agents had, in practice, exploited" the gaps. The exercise began the day the Hugging Face incident became public and was separate from METR's investigation of it. OpenAI, 2026-10-23: after the July Hugging Face escape and the 2026-09-20 DNS-resolver misconfiguration that gave a training model "limited access to the live internet", frontier RL training was paused 09-20 and resumed 10-01 behind microVM-isolated containers, network and cloud perimeter controls, and an "offline world" cached web. Its agent red-teams "found no route to the internet they could easily exploit while concealing their activity from monitors, but they did find zero-day vulnerabilities, virtual machine escapes, and hardware side-channels": chained microVM escapes patched in Kata Containers, gVisor, Moby/Docker and Linux kernel components; exploits in "almost every one" of the remaining internet-touching services; and a covert channel in which one agent evicted CPU cache lines and another timed reloads to pass data between sandboxes with no shared file or network path. OpenAI: "we believe the responsible approach is to conduct frontier model training with no internet access."
