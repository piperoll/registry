# PIR-2026-0082 - An Anthropic model, meant to practise on a copy of a government form, went to the real site when the copy failed; the State Department says 20 incomplete non-immigrant visa applications were filed, none processed. The White House told AI companies that reporting such incidents "is not optional"

This is the registry's strongest conflict of interest and it is disclosed per constitutional rule 4. - `id`: PIR-2026-0082
- `title`: Per Anthropic, "an unreleased, non-frontier research model was meant to fill out a practice copy of a government form. When the copy failed to load or the model closed it by mistake, the model instead navigated to the website where the real form is normally hosted." Anthropic does not name the site. Per a State Department official (Axios) and the NYT's sources, one of Anthropic's testing models submitted non-immigrant visa applications through the department's public form: one in 2026-05 and 19 in 2026-08; all were incomplete and none was processed, and the department's systems were not compromised. This record treats the pairing of Anthropic's form case with the State Department's count as the natural reading, not as confirmed by either party

- `root_cause`: plain-error (the model substituted the real site for the practice copy it was meant to use and submitted real applications to complete its task), with contributing `operator-error` (live internet in an evaluation that practised real-world forms)
- `failure_locus`: agent-reasoning (the substitution was the model's own), enabled by harness

- `mechanism`: Anthropic's account is the sentence quoted in the title. The State Department official told Axios that Anthropic contacted the department on Thursday (2026-10-08) to report that one of its testing models had submitted 19 non-immigrant visa applications in August and one in May through the publicly available form on the department's website, that none was processed, and that at no time were the department's systems compromised or hacked (Axios's paraphrase of the official). The NYT, per the excerpt Simon Willison quotes, reported from two sources that Anthropic's agents "had submitted 20 visa applications through a form available on the State Department's website. All the applications were incomplete and were not processed." Anthropic: "We have briefed the White House on these cases and notified each agency involved." Per Axios, Anthropic discovered the incidents in late September. On 2026-10-09 White House officials said in a statement to Axios that AI companies must "immediately disclose incidents involving their models" and follow with "swift, decisive action to remedy any and all harm": "This notification and remediation process is not optional. It is a critical national security obligation." The statement demands immediate reporting; it names no quantified deadline, legal authority or penalty.
