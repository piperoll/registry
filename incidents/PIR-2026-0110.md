# PIR-2026-0110 - A founder reported that his read-only "CFO" Grok Bot agent posted his personal bank balances and monthly spending into his company's executive Slack channel instead of his private agent group chat; he attributes it to similarly named destinations and agents sharing one underlying connection (self-reported; ExplainX reports a Community Note alleging fabrication)

**Disclosure**: This record concerns a Grok Bot agent, an xAI product; it is drafted by Claude Fable 5.1, an Anthropic model - a competitor to xAI. The conflict is disclosed per PipeRoll constitutional rule 4. No claim here rests on the drafting model's judgement; every claim is attributed to the party that made it. This record is `status: draft` and is kept out of INDEX: the only account is the operator's own, the supporting evidence is screenshots of the agent's dialogue rather than an independently verifiable Slack audit trail, ExplainX reports that a Community Note on the original post alleges fabrication (the note itself was not independently verified here), and no xAI statement has been located. An independent scope call was obtained before drafting from Codex, an OpenAI model (affiliation noted per rule 4); see Verification notes.

- `id`: PIR-2026-0110
- `title`: Shane Mac, CEO and co-founder of XMTP Labs, reported that a "CFO" agent he built on xAI's Grok Bot, with read-only access to his personal bank accounts and an instruction to message only him, posted his monthly financial audit - checking and savings balances and his largest expenses, including a barn he is building - into his company's "Exec-team" Slack channel instead of his private agent group chat, "My Personal Exec Team". By his account the agent "confused the destination": he characterises the two channels as having "the same name", and says his several Grok Bot agents ran on the same underlying connections. His head of product flagged it; Mac had already deleted it. Posted ~2026-10-06; unverified, and disputed by a reported Community Note
- `date_occurred`: 2026-10-01 (Mac's interview: "It was Thursday, October 1")
- `date_detected`: 2026-10-01 (same day; Mac's head of product sent him a Slack DM pointing out the post)
- `date_disclosed`: 2026-10-06 (Shane Mac's post on X; a Business Insider "as told to" interview followed 2026-10-09)
- `status`: draft (single-party self-report; screenshots of agent dialogue only, no independently authenticated Slack record; ExplainX reports a Community Note on the X post alleging fabrication, which could not be verified here; no xAI statement located; the operator sells agent-permission tooling, a disclosed commercial interest. Lifts to corroborated only with independently verifiable evidence, such as an authenticated Slack record or an xAI statement; moves to disputed if verified counter-evidence emerges)

### The agent
- `agent_description`: A personal-finance "CFO" agent that Mac built on xAI's Grok Bot around the end of August 2026 to produce a monthly review of his finances. Per Mac he gave it read-only access to his personal checking and savings accounts and instructed it to send messages only to him through Grok Bot; it reported into a group chat of his personal agents he named "My Personal Exec Team." He had also connected his work Slack through a separate Grok Bot agent. Per Mac, his several agents, though they felt separate, "are actually all using the same connections."
- `operator_type`: individual (Mac, running personal agents for himself)
- `autonomy_level`: autonomous-within-policy (the agent ran a scheduled monthly task on its own; Mac set it up and received the output)
- `model_stack`: Grok Bot (xAI; Business Insider's interview and Mac use "Grok Bot"; some secondary coverage refers to the maker as "SpaceXAI" and some as "xAI")
- `harness`: xAI's Grok Bot agent platform, with multiple user-defined agents sharing one underlying set of account connections (bank, Slack) per Mac's account

### Authority
- `authority_scope`: read access to Mac's personal checking and savings accounts (read-only, per Mac); the ability to post messages into Slack channels reachable through the connected Slack account, including his employer's
- `funds_at_risk_usd`: 0 (read-only financial access; no funds could be moved)
- `blast_radius`: one org (the members of XMTP Labs' "Exec-team" Slack channel, who saw Mac's personal financial details)

### The failure
- `root_cause`: tool-error (on Mac's account, which is unverified: the agent sent its report to the wrong Slack destination - "It was doing exactly what I told it to do. But it confused the destination" - because the destinations were similarly named; he calls them "the same name"). Contributing `operator-error` on the same account (Slack connected through one agent without anticipating that his agents shared one connection)
- `failure_locus`: unknown (Mac attributes the cause to the Grok Bot platform's shared connection layer and destination resolution, and says xAI's own investigation found it there; that is a single operator's report of a vendor investigation, not a vendor statement, so the locus is held provisional: harness on his account, operator-config contributing)
- `exploitation_status`: in-wild-malfunction (a real agent in ordinary use sent real data to the wrong place; no adversary)
- `mechanism`: Per Mac's account (Business Insider "as told to", read in full by this session; see Evidence). Unverified throughout. Mac set the CFO agent to answer monthly questions about balances, expenses, recurring costs and anything suspicious, with read-only bank access and an instruction to message only him; its reports went to his private "My Personal Exec Team" group chat. When the first monthly audit ran, it posted to his employer's "Exec-team" Slack channel instead. His head of product DMed him: "Hey, heads-up. I think you meant to post the XMTP bank balance." The post showed his checking balance, his savings balance and his largest monthly expenses, including the barn he is building. Mac asked the agent why it had sent the report to the company; it apologised and offered to delete the message, which he had already deleted. Per Mac, "When the Grok team investigated what had happened, they found the answer. The CFO agent didn't have psychosis. It was doing exactly what I told it to do. But it confused the destination," because, he says, his agents share one underlying connection and "the channels had the same name" (they were "My Personal Exec Team" and "Exec-team"). Mac says xAI then required users to grant explicit permission before an agent can move information to another channel and "implemented and shipped a solution" the night before the interview. Mac afterwards disconnected all his personal connections. None of this has independent confirmation.
- `adversary_present`: no (no attacker; a destination-resolution failure in ordinary use)

### Impact
- `severity`: loss (if the disclosure occurred as described: private financial data - two account balances and itemised personal spending - shown to the operator's colleagues; a realised, non-monetary loss rather than a near-miss, since the exposure itself is the event; no dollar figure attaches)
- `direct_loss_usd`: unknown (a privacy disclosure to colleagues; no monetary loss reported and none inherent)
- `indirect_loss_usd`: unknown
- `downtime`: none
- `data_exposure`: as reported by Mac: his personal checking balance, savings balance and largest monthly expenses, posted to XMTP Labs' executive Slack channel until he deleted it. ExplainX reports the audit appeared at 8:40 a.m. and stayed about two hours; that figure is not in Mac's interview and is not independently confirmed

### Detection and recovery
- `detected_by`: operator (Mac's head of product noticed the post and flagged it to him the same day)
- `time_to_detect`: same day (minutes to hours; not precisely stated)
- `time_to_recover`: Mac deleted the message after it was flagged; he then disconnected all his personal account connections
- `remediation`: Mac deleted the post and disconnected his personal connections (bank, Slack, Google, calendars, Stripe). Per Mac, xAI shipped a change requiring explicit user permission before an agent moves information to another channel; this is Mac's account and is not confirmed by xAI
- `structural_fix`: per Mac, xAI's permission change for cross-channel transfers (unconfirmed by xAI); on the user side, separating personal and work connections
- `controls_that_worked`: none prevented the disclosure; the read-only scope on the bank accounts limited the event to a data disclosure rather than a funds action, and a colleague caught it quickly
