# PIR-2026-0031 - Benavides v. Tesla: $243M verdict over fatal Autopilot crash, upheld post-trial

- `id`: PIR-2026-0031
- `title`: Federal jury holds Tesla 33% liable for a fatal 2019 Autopilot crash; $242.57M against Tesla survives post-trial motions
- `date_occurred`: 2019-04-25 (crash, Key Largo FL)
- `date_detected`: immediate (fatal collision)
- `date_disclosed`: jury verdict 2025-08-01 (S.D. Fla., Miami); verdict upheld 2026-02-20 (Judge Beth Bloom); appellate review continuing
- `status`: corroborated (verdict and post-trial ruling on public record; dollar amount not final pending appeal)

### The agent
- `agent_description`: Tesla Model S with Autopilot engaged - Level-2 driver-assist (lane keeping + traffic-aware cruise) marketed under branding the jury found overstated its capability; human driver remains the formal operator.
- `operator_type`: individual (driver) atop an enterprise-provided automation stack
- `autonomy_level`: human-on-the-loop (L2: automation steers/accelerates continuously, human nominally supervising)
- `model_stack`: Tesla Autopilot (2019-era hardware/software; exact version not public)
- `harness`: Tesla vehicle firmware
- `failure_locus`: shared - agent-reasoning/design (no enforcement of the operational design domain; no stop-sign response) and operator (distracted driver, 67% of fault)

### The failure
- `root_cause`: primary `plain-error` (no adversary; the automation continued at speed through an intersection it was not designed for); contributing `operator-error` (driver inattention; use outside the operational design domain that the design permitted)
- `mechanism`: Driver George McGee, with Autopilot engaged on a road outside its design domain, dropped his phone and looked away. The car went through the intersection at ~62 mph and struck a parked Chevrolet Tahoe, killing 22-year-old Naibel Benavides Leon and severely injuring Dillon Angulo. The Miami federal jury (2025-08-01) allocated 33% of fault to Tesla, resting on Tesla permitting Autopilot use outside its operational design domain and overstating its capability. Award against Tesla: $242.57M - $19.47M compensatory to the Benavides estate + $23.1M compensatory to Angulo (Tesla's 33% share of ~$129M total compensatory) + $200M punitive. On 2026-02-20 Judge Beth Bloom denied Tesla's post-trial motions, ruling the evidence "more than supports" the verdict.
