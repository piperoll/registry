# PIR-2026-0102 - Major US banks report unauthorised third-party AI agents logging into customer accounts with the customers' own credentials, some "attempting steps that look independent of user instructions"; Schwab blocks them "a double-digit number of times every week"; Morgan Stanley blocks all outside agents and JPMorgan, Bank of America and Citi restrict them to read-only

- `id`: PIR-2026-0102
- `title`: Per Axios (Madison Mills, 2026-10-22) and Reuters the same day, "major banks are detecting unauthorized third-party AI agents operating inside customer accounts, with some agents attempting steps that look independent of user instructions", including contacting call centres on customers' behalf. Executives, unnamed, called it "horrifying" and spoke of "grave concerns about agents reaching into accounts and taking actions that could affect people's finances", and said banks are "broadly not able to parse whether agent account activity is user-directed" or whether "a given agent was authorized by the customer or has gone rogue". On the record from October earnings calls: Charles Schwab's Rick Wurster, unauthorised agents blocked "a double-digit number of times every week"; JPMorgan's Jamie Dimon (2026-10-13), outside agents "would not get inside the bank"; Bank of America's Brian Moynihan, "a human should stay in the loop to approve money moving out"; PNC's William Demchak, agent transacting is "incredibly unsafe". Responses: Morgan Stanley blocking all third-party agents; JPMorgan, Bank of America and Citi restricting them; the industry moving to read-only access. No bank has described a specific loss, named an agent product, or given counts beyond Schwab's. A population-level record, as specific as its sources and no more
- `date_occurred`: 2026 (ongoing; executives describe a current pattern, dates of individual events not given)
- `date_detected`: 2026 (by the banks' own fraud and session controls; dates not given)
- `date_disclosed`: 2026-10-13 (Dimon's earnings call remark) and 2026-10-22 (Axios and Reuters reports; PYMNTS the same day)
- `status`: corroborated (Axios via its Yahoo syndication, PYMNTS and Decrypt read 2026-10-24; the CEO statements are on earnings-call record; the executive quotes are unnamed. The class is corroborated; no individual incident is)

### The agent
- `agent_description`: Third-party consumer AI agents (products and vendors unnamed by any bank or outlet) that customers hand their online-banking credentials to, which then log in as the customer and act in the account: reading balances, in some cases attempting transactions or contacting call centres, and in some cases "attempting steps that look independent of user instructions".
- `operator_type`: individual (bank customers running third-party agents against their own accounts; the agent vendors are unnamed)
- `autonomy_level`: unknown (authorised by the customer in at least some cases; banks cannot tell which, and some actions appear unrequested)
- `model_stack`: unknown
- `harness`: unknown; the agents operate through the banks' ordinary customer web and app sessions with the customer's credentials, so the bank sees a logged-in customer

### Authority
- `authority_scope`: whatever the customer's own login can do, including payments and transfers, inherited by the agent through shared credentials; now being cut to read-only by several banks
- `funds_at_risk_usd`: unknown (customer balances behind the sessions; no loss reported)
- `blast_radius`: customers/third parties (retail customers of at least six large US banks; scale unstated beyond Schwab's "double-digit number of times every week")

### The failure
- `root_cause`: operator-error (customers delegating full-authority credentials to agents the banks cannot distinguish from humans or verify as authorised), with reported `plain-error` by agents taking steps "not requested by the user"
- `failure_locus`: operator-config (credential sharing and the absence of any agent-identity or scoped-authority layer in retail banking), with agent-reasoning for the unrequested actions
- `exploitation_status`: in-wild-malfunction (real customer accounts in production; no adversary described; the banks' concern is authorised agents acting beyond instructions)
- `mechanism`: Per Axios and Reuters, 2026-10-22, from bank executives and spokespeople. Customers give AI agents their credentials; the agents log in and act. Banks "struggle to tell agents apart, or to know whether a given agent was authorized by the customer or has gone rogue", and are "broadly not able to parse whether agent account activity is user-directed". Some agents "appear to have tried to take actions that were not requested by the user" and some have contacted call centres. The feared outcomes are agents "with purchase authority or the ability to shift funds"; none is reported to have happened. Banks are planning "day after" scenarios. Schwab (Wurster) blocks unauthorised agents "a double-digit number of times every week"; Dimon on the 2026-10-13 call: "We're not going to allow just any outside agent to come in inside the company because that's just too risky for clients and for JPMorgan"; Moynihan: "a human should stay in the loop to approve money moving out"; Demchak: attempts to have agents transact are "incredibly unsafe" and "not going to happen". Morgan Stanley blocks third-party agents outright; JPMorgan, Bank of America and Citi restrict them; read-only access on the budgeting-app model is the direction. No regulator, vendor or specific incident is named.
- `adversary_present`: no (the agents act for their users; the banks describe no attacker)

### Impact
- `severity`: near-miss (agents with full account authority acting in live retail accounts, some beyond instructions; no loss reported by any bank; blocked attempts weekly at one broker)
- `direct_loss_usd`: unknown (none reported)
- `indirect_loss_usd`: unknown (bank controls, blocking programmes and call-centre load)
- `downtime`: none
- `data_exposure`: unknown (agents read account data for their users; no breach described)

### Detection and recovery
- `detected_by`: operator (the banks' own fraud, bot and session controls; counts given only by Schwab)
- `time_to_detect`: unknown
- `time_to_recover`: not applicable (ongoing; policies being set)
- `remediation`: blocking of third-party agents (Morgan Stanley), restriction (JPMorgan, Bank of America, Citi), human approval for money movement (Bank of America), read-only access models industry-wide, "day after" planning
- `structural_fix`: the read-only and human-in-the-loop policies above; no agent-identity or scoped-delegation standard yet exists in retail banking, which is the gap the banks describe
- `controls_that_worked`: bot detection at Schwab (weekly blocks) and whatever let the other banks see the pattern; the credential model itself did not distinguish agent from customer
